## पंचतंत्र / हितोपदेश

### एकीचे बळ  `ekiche-bal-kabutare`

- **Ages:** 2-3, 4-5, 6-7 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** animals
- **Names:** चित्रग्रीव, हिरण्यक
- **Plot:**
  1. A flock of doves led by their wise king Chitragriva sees rice scattered under a tree and flies down to eat.
  2. It is a hunter's net; all the doves are caught and start flapping in panic.
  3. Chitragriva tells them: if we all fly up together at the same moment, we can lift the net.
  4. Together they rise into the sky carrying the whole net while the hunter watches, amazed.
  5. They fly to Chitragriva's friend, the mouse Hiranyaka, who nibbles the net and frees every dove — the king last.
- **Value:** Together we are strong; a good leader thinks of everyone first.
- **Softening:** The hunter is never frightening — just a man who goes home with an empty net.

### माकड आणि मगर  `makad-ani-magar`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** animals
- **Plot:**
  1. A monkey lives in a jamun tree by a river and shares sweet jamuns with a crocodile every day; they become friends.
  2. The crocodile takes jamuns home; his wife says a monkey who eats such sweet fruit must have a very sweet heart, and she wants to eat it.
  3. The crocodile invites the monkey home for a feast and carries him on his back across the river.
  4. Mid-river the crocodile admits the plan. The monkey stays calm and says: oh no, I left my heart up in the jamun tree — take me back and I'll fetch it.
  5. Back at the bank the monkey leaps into his tree and says a heart lives in the chest, not on a branch — and a friend who plans to harm you is no friend.
- **Value:** Keep a calm mind in trouble; trust must be earned.
- **Softening:** No eating happens; the wife's wish is told lightly. The monkey ends wiser, not cruel.

### चतुर ससा आणि सिंह  `sasa-ani-sinha`

- **Ages:** 4-5, 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** animals
- **Plot:**
  1. A greedy lion frightens the forest; the animals agree to send him one animal a day so he stops chasing everyone.
  2. One day it is a small hare's turn. He walks slowly, thinking, and arrives very late.
  3. He tells the angry lion that another lion on the way said he is the real king of this forest.
  4. The lion demands to see him; the hare leads him to a deep well. The lion sees his own reflection and roars; the well roars back.
  5. The lion leaps at the "other lion" (older listeners) or flees in fright and leaves the forest for ever (younger listeners). The animals live in peace.
- **Value:** Cleverness beats size and strength.
- **Softening:** Ages 4–5: the echo scares the lion away for ever — no falling into the well. No animal is ever eaten on-screen; the "one a day" deal is mentioned only briefly.

### बोलका कासव  `bolka-kasav`

- **Ages:** 4-5, 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** animals
- **Plot:**
  1. A chatty tortoise lives in a lake with two goose friends. A long dry summer starts emptying the lake.
  2. The geese will fly to a full lake far away. They hold a stick in their beaks; the tortoise bites the middle — but must not open his mouth.
  3. High over a village, children point and laugh: look, the geese are carrying a tortoise!
  4. The tortoise cannot stop himself — he opens his mouth to answer, and lets go of the stick.
  5. He tumbles down and lands with a big splash in a soft muddy pond, safe but shaken, and learns that sometimes silence is wisdom. The geese come down and help him on.
- **Value:** Think before you speak.
- **Softening:** In the old version the tortoise dies — here he lands safely in soft mud. Never make the fall frightening.

### चार मित्र  `char-mitra`

- **Ages:** 4-5, 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** animals
- **Plot:**
  1. A crow, a mouse, a tortoise and a deer are close friends who meet by a lake every day.
  2. One day the deer doesn't come. The crow flies to look and finds him caught in a hunter's net; the crow carries the mouse there and the mouse nibbles him free.
  3. The slow tortoise has followed — and the returning hunter catches him in a bag instead.
  4. The friends make a plan: the deer lies still near the path as if hurt; the hunter puts down the bag and goes after the deer.
  5. The mouse nibbles the bag open, the tortoise slips into the water, the deer springs up and runs. The four friends meet again, laughing.
- **Value:** True friends think and act together to help each other.
- **Softening:** The deer only pretends to be hurt; no harm comes to anyone.

### हत्ती आणि उंदीर  `hatti-ani-undir`

- **Ages:** 2-3, 4-5, 6-7 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** animals
- **Plot:**
  1. Many mice live happily in an old ruined village by a lake path.
  2. A herd of elephants walks through every day to drink, accidentally squashing the mice's homes.
  3. The mouse king politely asks the elephant king to take another path. The elephants agree kindly; the mouse king promises: one day we will help you too. The elephants smile.
  4. Later the elephants are caught in strong ropes and nets set by the king's trappers. One elephant calls for the mice.
  5. Hundreds of mice nibble through every rope and the elephants are free. Big and small are friends for ever.
- **Value:** No one is too small to help; kindness comes back.
- **Softening:** Ages 2–3: the elephants just wreck the mice's homes by mistake — nobody is hurt.

### निळा कोल्हा  `nila-kolha`

- **Ages:** 4-5, 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** animals
- **Plot:**
  1. A hungry jackal wanders into a village, is chased by dogs, and falls into a big tub of blue dye.
  2. He comes out bright blue. Back in the forest the animals have never seen such a creature and are scared of him.
  3. The jackal says the forest gods sent him to be their king. The lion, tiger and elephant serve him; he even sends the other jackals away.
  4. One evening the jackals howl together far away (कोल्हेकुई). Without thinking, he lifts his nose and howls along with them!
  5. Everyone sees he is just a jackal. Laughed out of his throne, he runs away, and learns it is better to be yourself.
- **Value:** Pretending doesn't last; be yourself.
- **Softening:** In the old version he is killed — here he is laughed away and learns.

### चतुर कावळा आणि साप  `kavla-ani-saap`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** animals
- **Plot:**
  1. A crow couple nests in a tree; a snake in a hole at its foot keeps stealing their eggs.
  2. A wise friend (a jackal) tells the crow: use your brains, not your beak.
  3. The crow flies to the palace where the queen is bathing, picks up her shining necklace and flies off slowly so the guards follow.
  4. He drops the necklace into the snake's hole. The guards dig it out and chase the snake far away.
  5. The crow family lives in peace and their eggs hatch.
- **Value:** Clever thinking solves what strength cannot.
- **Softening:** Guards chase the snake far away — they do not kill it.

## जातक कथा

### चंद्रावरचा ससा  `chandravarcha-sasa`

- **Ages:** 4-5, 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** bedtime
- **Plot:**
  1. A hare, a monkey, a jackal and an otter are friends; on a special day they decide to give food to anyone in need.
  2. An old, tired traveller comes. The monkey brings fruit, the otter brings fish, the jackal brings milk and curd.
  3. The hare only eats grass — what can he give? He decides to give everything he has: himself.
  4. Before he can, the old man stops him and reveals he is a god who came to test their kindness.
  5. To remember the hare's big heart, he draws the hare's picture on the moon — look up tonight and you can see it.
- **Value:** Giving with your whole heart.
- **Softening:** In the old version the hare jumps into a fire. Here he only offers, and is stopped at once — no fire. End softly, looking at the moon.

## अकबर–बिरबल

### बिरबलाची खिचडी  `birbalachi-khichdi`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** funny
- **Names:** अकबर, बिरबल
- **Plot:**
  1. On a freezing winter day Emperor Akbar announces a prize for anyone who stands all night in the cold lake water.
  2. A poor man does it. In the morning Akbar asks how; the man says he kept looking at a lamp burning far away in the palace.
  3. Akbar says: so the lamp's warmth helped you — no prize. The man goes home sad.
  4. Birbal doesn't come to court. Akbar finds him sitting by a tiny fire with a pot of khichdi hung high up a pole: it's still cooking!
  5. Akbar laughs: how can it cook so far from the fire? Birbal: the same way that lamp warmed the man. Akbar understands and gives the man his prize.
- **Value:** Fairness; being clever to help someone treated unfairly.
- **Softening:** The lake scene is brief and not dangerous-sounding; the man is fine.

### न पुसता रेषा लहान  `birbal-resha`

- **Ages:** 4-5, 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** funny
- **Names:** अकबर, बिरबल
- **Plot:**
  1. Akbar draws a line on the floor and challenges his court: make this line shorter — without rubbing out even a bit of it.
  2. The courtiers scratch their heads; one tries to cover it with his hand; another gives up.
  3. Birbal quietly draws a longer line right beside it.
  4. Now the first line looks short! Akbar laughs and praises him.
- **Value:** Thinking differently; you grow by doing better, not by making others small.

### शहरात कावळे किती?  `birbal-kavle-kiti`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** funny
- **Names:** अकबर, बिरबल
- **Plot:**
  1. Akbar asks Birbal a tricky question: how many crows are there in our city?
  2. Birbal thinks for a moment and gives a big exact number.
  3. Akbar: and if I count and find fewer? Birbal: then some crows have gone to visit relatives in other towns.
  4. And if there are more? Then relatives from other towns have come visiting! Akbar can't stop laughing.
- **Value:** Quick wit and a cheerful mind.

## तेनाली राम

### तेनालीची विहीर आणि चोर  `tenali-vihiritle-chor`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** funny
- **Names:** तेनाली राम
- **Plot:**
  1. Tenali Raman notices thieves hiding in the bushes near his house one night.
  2. He says loudly to his wife: thieves are about — let's hide our valuables in this box and drop it in the well. He drops a box full of stones.
  3. All night the thieves pull bucket after bucket of water out of the well to reach the box; the water flows into Tenali's dry garden.
  4. At dawn they find only stones; Tenali calls out "thank you for watering my garden!" and they run away.
- **Value:** A calm, clever mind protects you better than fear.
- **Softening:** The thieves are silly, never scary.

### शहाणपणाचं मडकं  `tenali-shahanpanache-madke`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** folk tale · **Shelf:** funny
- **Names:** तेनाली राम, कृष्णदेवराय
- **Plot:**
  1. A neighbouring king sends a message to King Krishnadevaraya: send us a pot full of wisdom.
  2. The court is puzzled. Tenali asks for a few months and goes to his garden.
  3. He puts a tiny pumpkin, still on its vine, inside a narrow-necked pot and lets it grow until it fills the pot.
  4. He sends the pot with a note: here is a pot of wisdom — take the wisdom out without breaking the pot or cutting the wisdom, and please send the pot back.
  5. The other king understands he has met a cleverer mind, and sends friendship instead of riddles.
- **Value:** Patience and clever thinking.
- **Must keep:** Versions of this story differ; keep the pumpkin-in-pot idea.

### गणपतीची पृथ्वीप्रदक्षिणा  `ganpati-pradakshina`

- **Ages:** 2-3, 4-5, 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** myth (told with respect) · **Shelf:** festivals
- **Names:** गणपती, कार्तिकेय, शंकर, पार्वती
- **Plot:**
  1. Shankar and Parvati have one special fruit and two sons. They say: whoever goes round the whole world first gets the fruit.
  2. Kartikeya jumps on his fast peacock and flies off at once.
  3. Ganpati's ride is a little mouse. He thinks for a moment.
  4. He walks slowly around his mother and father, folds his hands and says: you are my whole world.
  5. Shankar and Parvati are delighted and give Ganpati the fruit. When Kartikeya returns, everyone shares the happy moment.
- **Value:** Love for parents; thinking wisely.
- **Softening:** No sulking or quarrel at the end — keep it warm.

### गणपती आणि चंद्र  `ganpati-ani-chandra`

- **Ages:** 4-5, 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** myth (told with respect) · **Shelf:** festivals
- **Names:** गणपती
- **Plot:**
  1. On his festival day Ganpati eats lots of modaks and rides home on his mouse under the night sky.
  2. The mouse sees a snake, gets startled and stumbles; Ganpati tumbles off and modaks roll everywhere.
  3. The Moon laughs at him from the sky.
  4. Ganpati says: since you laughed at someone who fell, people won't look at you on this night. The Moon is sorry and apologises.
  5. Ganpati forgives him and keeps it to just that one night — which is why on Ganesh Chaturthi people avoid looking at the moon.
- **Value:** Don't laugh at someone who falls; saying sorry heals.
- **Softening:** No injury — just rolling modaks. The "curse" is gentle and quickly softened.

### बाळ हनुमान आणि सूर्य  `hanuman-ani-surya`

- **Names:** हनुमान, इंद्र, वायू

- **Plot:**
  1. Changdev is a famous yogi, very proud of his powers. He comes to meet young Dnyaneshwar riding a tiger and holding a snake as a whip.
  2. Dnyaneshwar and his brothers and sister Muktabai are sitting on an old wall in the sun.

### इंद्रायणीत तरलेली गाथा  `tukaram-gatha`

- **Ages:** 8-9 · **Framing:** legend — Aaji says "असं सांगतात" · **Shelf:** festivals
- **Names:** संत तुकाराम, इंद्रायणी
- **Plot:**
  1. Sant Tukaram writes abhangs — simple Marathi songs about God and goodness — and everyone loves to sing them.
  2. Some people do not like it and tell him to throw his notebooks into the Indrayani river. He does, quietly.

### बाळ आणि शेंगांची टरफलं  `tilak-shengachi-tarphale`

- **Ages:** 4-5, 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** legend — Aaji says "असं सांगतात" · **Shelf:** adventure
- **Names:** बाळ, लोकमान्य टिळक
- **Plot:**
  1. Little Bal is a student in a school. One day peanut shells are found all over the classroom floor.
  2. The teacher asks who did it; nobody owns up, so the teacher says everyone must pick up the shells.
  3. Bal doesn't move. He says: I didn't eat the peanuts, so I won't pick up the shells.
  4. He accepts the consequence calmly rather than say something untrue.
  5. Aaji ends: that boy grew up to be Lokmanya Tilak, who always spoke the truth fearlessly.
- **Value:** Standing by the truth calmly.
- **Must keep:** "मी शेंगा खाल्ल्या नाहीत, मी टरफलं उचलणार नाही." In the first paragraph make clear that बाळ is the boy's NAME ("सगळे त्याला बाळ म्हणायचे"); never use बाळ to mean a baby, and never write "लहान बाळ".
- **Softening:** The consequence is mild (sent home / a note to his father). The teacher is not a villain.

### सावित्रीबाईंची दुसरी साडी  `savitribai-dusri-sadi`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** true — only these events · **Shelf:** adventure

- **Plot:**
  1. Long ago in Pune, girls did not go to school. Savitribai Phule, with Jotiba Phule, starts a school for girls and becomes its teacher.

- **Softening:** No battles. Always say छत्रपती शिवाजी महाराज in full when naming him as a grown-up. Do not mention any teacher or guardian figure — tell it through Jijau only.

- **Names:** मोहन, महात्मा गांधी

  1. An inspector visits young Mohan's school and gives the class a spelling test of five English words; one is "kettle".
  2. Mohan spells kettle wrong. His teacher signals him to copy from the boy next to him.
  3. Mohan doesn't copy. Everyone else gets all words right; he doesn't — and he doesn't mind.

### पुस्तकांचं घर  `ambedkar-pustakanche-ghar`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** true — only these events · **Shelf:** adventure
- **Names:** बाबासाहेब आंबेडकर
- **Plot:**
  1. Bhim loves reading more than anything; even when life is hard he studies with all his heart.
  2. He grows up and studies in India and far away in other countries, reading late into the night.
  3. He collects so many books that he builds a house in Mumbai especially for them — Rajgruha.
  4. He uses all that learning to help write India's Constitution, so that everyone is treated equally.
- **Value:** Learning can change your life and the world.
- **Must keep:** His home Rajgruha in Dadar, Mumbai was built to hold his library.
- **Softening:** Keep it about books and learning; if hardship is mentioned, say only that some people treated him unfairly — no details, no blaming any group.

### पक्ष्यांचे पंख  `kalam-pakshyanche-pankh`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** true — only these events · **Shelf:** adventure
- **Names:** अब्दुल कलाम, रामेश्वरम
- **Plot:**
  1. In Rameswaram, by the sea, young Abdul helps his family by delivering newspapers early in the morning.
  2. One day his teacher takes the class to the seashore to watch birds fly, explaining how wings lift them.
  3. Abdul watches the birds for a long time and decides: I want to learn about flying.
  4. Aaji ends: that boy became Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, who built rockets for India and became President.
- **Value:** Curiosity and hard work make dreams fly.

### नरेनचं चाफ्याचं झाड  `vivekananda-champyache-jhad`

- **Ages:** 6-7, 8-9 · **Framing:** legend — Aaji says "असं सांगतात" · **Shelf:** adventure
- **Names:** नरेन, स्वामी विवेकानंद
- **Plot:**
  1. Little Naren and his friends love playing on a champa tree in a neighbour's garden.
  2. Worried they will break the branches, the old neighbour says a ghost lives in that tree.
  3. The friends get scared, but Naren says: if there were a ghost, it would have met us long ago! Let's think, not just believe.
  4. Aaji ends: that Naren grew up to be Swami Vivekananda, who taught people to be fearless and to think.
- **Value:** Think for yourself; don't be ruled by fear.
- **Softening:** Ghost talk stays light and funny, never scary. Imitable danger: no climbing high — they play on the low branches.
